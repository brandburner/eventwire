1. Tone of voice

GOV.UK content must be: specific, informative, clear and concise, brisk but not terse, incisive but human, serious but not pompous, emotionless (no subjective adjectives or spin).

  • Write conversationally, one-to-one, with the authority of someone who can actively help.
  • Active voice, not passive.
  • Address the user as 'you'. Gender-neutral third person ('they', never 'he or she').
  • No 'please' or 'please note' — including in instructions.
  • No block capitals for emphasis.
  • No offensive language anywhere, including attachments.
  • Only use 'we' after the organisation's full name has appeared (except 'About us' pages and policies).

2. Clear language

  • Plain English is mandatory, including for specialist audiences (specialists prefer it too).
  • Short, common words: 'buy' not 'purchase', 'help' not 'assist', 'about' not 'approximately'.
  • Specialist terms are allowed when needed — explain them in plain English at first use (for example 'bona vacantia').
  • Use the words users search for (check search-term data).
  • Avoid words ending in '-ion' and '-ment' where a verb is simpler.
  • Contractions: positive contractions like 'you'll' are fine. Avoid negative contractions ('can't', 'don't' → 'cannot', 'do not') and complex ones ('should've', 'could've', 'would've', 'they've').
  • Sentences: try to split anything over 25 words.
  • Paragraphs: no more than 5 sentences each.

Requirement language (must / need / can)

  • 'must' — legal requirement: 'Your employer must pay you the National Minimum Wage (NMW)'.
  • 'legal requirement' / 'legally entitled' / 'legally responsible' — when 'must' lacks emphasis.
  • 'need' — legal-but-administrative steps with no criminal repercussions: 'You will need to provide copies of your marriage certificate'.
  • 'can' — optional actions. Avoid 'You may be able to'.

Words to avoid (plain English is mandatory)

agenda, advance, collaborate (→ work with), combat, commit/pledge (→ plan to x), counter, deliver (only pizzas and post are delivered), deploy (unless military/software), dialogue, disincentivise, empower, facilitate (say how), focus (→ work on), foster (unless children), impact (unless collision), incentivise, initiate (→ start), key (unless it unlocks; → important), land (unless aircraft), leverage (unless financial), liaise, overarching, progress (→ work on), promote (unless ad campaign), robust (unless an object), slim down, streamline (→ simplify), strengthening (unless structures), tackle (unless rugby/fishing), transform (describe the change), utilise (→ use).

Avoid metaphors: drive, drive out, going/moving forward, in order to (delete it), hub/portal/one-stop shop (→ website, service), ring fencing.

3. Structure

  • Frontload everything: most important information first, inverted pyramid.
  • Headings: descriptive (never 'Introduction'), frontloaded, active (start with a verb where possible), removable without losing sense, never questions, no unexplained technical terms.
  • No footnotes — fold important content into body text, drop the rest.
  • Do not repeat the page summary in the first paragraph.
  • No FAQs. Content written from user needs does not need them.

Bullet points

  • Always use a lead-in line, and more than one bullet.
  • Bullets must run on grammatically from the lead-in line.
  • Lower case at the start of each bullet.
  • Maximum one sentence per bullet (use commas or dashes to expand).
  • No 'and'/'or' after bullets, no semicolons, no full stop after the last bullet.
  • Do not make a whole long phrase the link.
  • If a list might look exhaustive but is not, fix the list rather than caveat it: check completeness, list the most common conditions, broaden terms, or route to specialist content.

Numbered steps

Use numbered steps (not bullets) to guide a user through a process. No lead-in line required. Each step is a complete sentence ending in a full stop. Links and formatting are allowed in steps.

4. Titles and summaries

Titles: 65 characters or less (including spaces); unique across the site; make sense by themselves; frontloaded with common search terms; use a colon to break up long titles; no dashes or slashes; no trailing full stop; not questions; no content type ('guidance', 'consultation'); active verb for transactional pages ('Submit your business expenses'), present participle for guidance pages ('Submitting your business expenses'); no organisation name or date unless needed for uniqueness.

Summaries: 160 characters or less, ending with a full stop; active, include a verb; do not repeat title words; no 'This consultation is about…'; introduce with 'How…', 'What…', 'When…' where useful.

5. Links

  • Descriptive link text, frontloaded — never 'click here' or 'more'.
  • Start link text with a verb if it begins a task ('Send a tax return'); use the destination page's title/topic if it leads to information.
  • One-word link text should be at least 3 syllables (clickability).
  • Same destination → identical or equivalent link text; never the same text for different destinations.
  • Flag departures: 'Find a counsellor on Counselling Directory' (off GOV.UK), '(in French)' (other language), '(PDF, 19.5KB)' (direct document link).
  • Links in context at point of use — no 'Further reading' dumps at the bottom, no link swamps, no repeated links to the same place.
  • No links in titles, summaries or subheadings.
  • Avoid anchor links (disorienting for assistive tech). If unavoidable cross-page, make text say where, what to do and why.
  • Link to online services first; offline alternatives after.
  • External links: specific page not homepage, no paywall/registration, accessible, justified if commercial.

6. A to Z style essentials

  • Abbreviations: explain at first use — 'Personal Independence Payment (PIP)', then PIP. No full stops (BBC not B.B.C.). Well-known ones need no explanation: EU, GP, HMRC-style HM names, MOT, NHS, PAYE, UK, VAT, USA (not 'US'), and file types, qualifications, post-nominals.
  • Dates: 4 June 2017 (no comma); upper case months; use 'to' in all ranges — 'tax year 2025 to 2026', '10 November to 21 December' (never hyphens, slashes or dashes); 'on or before 6 September 2025' for deadlines.
  • Times: 10am to 11am; 5:30pm not 1730hrs; midday not 12pm; prefer '11:59pm' over 'midnight' for deadlines; 'Monday to Friday, 9am to 5pm'; add 'UK time' for international users.
  • Numbers: write numbers as numerals, including 2 to 9. Use 'one' in prose unless a numeral makes more sense (steps, lists, points). Spell out a number that starts a sentence. Comma in 9,000+. Ordinals: first to ninth, then 10th. 0.5 not .5. '500 to 900', not '500-900'.
  • Money: £75 not £75.00; pence written out ('4 pence per minute'); '£138 million' — never abbreviate million/billion to m/b; no '£0.2 billion' outside a sequence of larger amounts.
  • Percentages: 50% (always % with a number); 'per cent' two words when written out.
  • Measurements: 3,500kg (no space); spell out multi-word units at first use; Celsius: 37°C.
  • eg, etc, ie: do not use. Replace with 'for example', 'such as', 'like', 'including', 'meaning', 'that is'.
  • Semicolons: do not use — split into separate sentences.
  • Italics: do not use. Single quotes for documents, schemes, publications.
  • Bold: only for interface elements in instructions ('Select Start'). Never for emphasis — frontload, use headings or bullets instead.
  • Quotes: single quotes for unusual terms (first mention), titles, interface words; double quotes only for direct speech.
  • Brackets: (round) for asides; do not use '(s)' for optional plurals — use the plural; [square] for clarifications in quotes/placeholders.
  • Capitalisation: sentence case everywhere, including titles. Proper nouns, specific schemes (Right to Buy), named benefits and taxes (Income Tax, Universal Credit, Statutory Sick Pay) take title case; 'government', 'minister', 'department' lower case generically.
  • 'sign in' not 'log in'; 'login' is not a noun (say what to enter).
  • 'fine' not 'financial penalty': 'You'll pay a £50 fine.' Describe what happens to the user, not what government calls it.
  • 'select' not 'click' for interfaces; 'page' not 'screen'.
  • Addresses (UK): each part on a new line, no trailing commas, country after postcode only if writing from abroad.
  • Telephone: 'Telephone:' or 'Mobile phone:'; spaced groups — 020 7946 0457.
  • Organisations: singular verb, 'they' as pronoun; definite article with full name but not abbreviation ('the Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency' but 'DVSA').
  • data is singular. Great Britain = England, Scotland and Wales — say so, and state if Northern Ireland is excluded. local council preferred over 'local authority' (never 'LA').
  • Inclusive language: 'disabled people' (not 'the disabled'); refer to ethnic minority groups individually, never BAME/BME; gender-neutral text; 'aged 50 years and over' not 'the over 50s'.
  • SMEs = small and medium-sized enterprises; SME singular.
  • References: no italics, single-quoted titles, abbreviations in full, 'and others' not 'et al', no full stops after initials.

7. Change notes

Public, emailed to subscribers. Required for substantive changes (new information users must act on, removed misleading guidance, changed fees or deadlines). Not for typos, style changes or routine link fixes. Must be specific, full sentences, frontloaded, and include the changed information itself where possible. Never 'Guidance updated.'

8. Things the 2026 guidance changed for this repo

These supersede older rules embedded in agents:

  1. Numbers: numerals throughout (including 2 to 9) — not 'spell out one to nine'.
  2. Abbreviations: never punctuate — 'UK' not 'U.K.', 'USA' not 'U.S.' or 'US'.
  3. Tax years in user-facing prose: 'tax year 2025 to 2026' — not '2025/26' or '2025-26'. (Internal snippet keys, filenames and QA metadata may keep the `2025/26` convention; convert at render/composition time.)
  4. Negative contractions: 'do not', 'cannot' — never 'don't', 'can't', even in examples.
  5. 'please' is banned in guidance register.
  6. eg/ie/etc are banned in user-facing content.
  7. Semicolons are banned.
  8. Requirement language: must (legal) / need (administrative) / can (optional) — see section 2.
